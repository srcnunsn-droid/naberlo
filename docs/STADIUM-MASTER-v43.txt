NATURAL DAYLIGHT AERIAL GRADE - MANDATORY FOR R0, E0, D0 AND EVERY DAYTIME AERIAL\
Image generators drift toward a bright, oversaturated postcard look, which immediately reads as fake. Counter it explicitly in every daytime aerial prompt:

- Light: soft natural daylight from a sun at moderate height (mid-morning or late afternoon) or a bright, thinly overcast sky, with gentle, directional shadows. No harsh midday glare.
- Sky: pale blue that fades to a hazy, almost white band near the horizon, with thin or scattered natural cloud. No deep saturated polarizer blue and no picture-perfect fluffy clouds.
- Atmospheric perspective: contrast, saturation and sharpness fall off steadily with distance, and the far skyline softens into blue-grey haze. Only the stadium and its immediate surroundings are in full focus.
- Color: true-to-life, slightly muted color as in an unedited aerial photograph: olive and dusty greens, weathered brick, grey roofs and asphalt, off-white surfaces. Club colors stay accurate but never neon.
- Tone: moderate contrast, open shadows with detail, white roofs and surfaces bright but never glowing or clipped. No HDR, no clarity or vibrance boost, no glow or bloom in daylight.
- Texture: natural fine detail with slight softness at distance and faint real sensor noise. No oversharpened edges or crunchy micro-detail.

NATURAL NIGHT PHOTO GRADE - MANDATORY FOR NB0, N0 AND EVERY NIGHT IMAGE\
A real night photograph is mostly dark. Light exists only where a real source puts it, and everything else falls into shadow with fine noise. Generators instead light the whole scene evenly, tint it orange or in the club color, make every edge glow and paint a flat, saturated navy sky, which is exactly why night images look artificial next to the daytime photograph. Counter it explicitly in every night prompt:

- Time and sky: full night, about 60-90 minutes after sunset. No sunset colors, no orange or pink horizon band and no dramatic or painterly clouds. The sky keeps the weather of the accepted daytime plate: an overcast day becomes a dark, nearly uniform grey sky faintly lit from below by city skyglow; a clear day becomes a dark, almost featureless blue-black sky that lightens only slightly toward the horizon from light pollution. Never a flat, saturated navy band and never a blue-hour postcard sky.
- Exposure hierarchy: the floodlit pitch and the stadium are the brightest parts of the frame. Main roads read as chains of lamp pools, residential streets are dim, rooftops are dark grey, trees and parks are near-black masses lit only directly under lamps, and car parks show pools of mast lighting with dark gaps between them. Roughly half of the frame or more sits in deep shadow.
- Light sources and color: match the city's real street lighting (modern neutral-white LED or older orange sodium), warm lit windows scattered irregularly with most windows dark, white headlights and red taillights as small points, and naturally mixed color temperatures. No single orange or red cast over the whole city.
- Club-colored light stays on the stadium: LED facades and roof edges are bright in the club color, but their spill falls off quickly across the immediate plaza and never tints the city, the sky or distant buildings.
- Optics and sensor: fine high-ISO noise in the shadows, slight overall softness, small natural glare around the brightest lamps and floodlights, highlight cores near white with colored edges on very bright LEDs, and distant lights softened by haze and merging together. No bloom wash, no glowing outlines, no HDR and no crisp detail all the way to the horizon.
- Real exposure: render it as a real drone night photograph exposed for the stadium, at roughly ISO 1600-3200 and 1/30-1/60 s. The city is underexposed, dark areas fall close to black, fine noise is visible and distant detail is slightly soft. Never expose the whole city as if it were lit by daylight.
- Irregular city lights: streetlights differ in brightness and spacing and some are out, main roads are brighter than side streets, and lit windows appear in irregular clusters while most houses are dark. Never a uniform carpet of identical orange dots.
- Stadium light physics: floodlights form a bright pool on the pitch and spill upward into the haze, creating a faint light dome above the roof. LED facades show their panel and pixel structure with slight brightness variation rather than a flat glowing color, and the brightest highlights clip to white. Through a glass or ETFE roof the floodlight structure and the bright pitch read clearly while the roof structure stays dark against them.
- Color: apart from the light sources, the image is slightly desaturated, with neutral, cool shadows. No warm orange wash over roofs, trees or roads.
- Ground: dry or damp according to the established weather. Never add a wet, mirror-like pavement as a default stylistic effect.
- People: lit only where lamps, screens or facades reach them, and darker silhouettes elsewhere. Phone screens and scarves add small accents.

## 27 - FACT LOCK AND ZERO-EMBARRASSMENT VERIFICATION

Viewer comments on earlier episodes repeatedly flagged wrong symbols and colors. One wrong crest, star, color, name or player is enough to turn a viral post into ridicule, so every club-identity fact passes this lock before it appears in any image or prompt. Keep the dossier internal and print it only on "DOĞRULA".\
FACT LOCK DOSSIER\
For each item record the value, source link, source date and status: verified, single-source or unverified. Critical items (marked *) need an official club source or two independent reliable sources.

1. Club* - official full name and short name exactly as the club writes them, including local characters such as Ş, İ, Ğ, Ü, Ö, Ç, ñ or é, plus nickname, city and founding year.
2. Crest* - the CURRENT official crest version as of REFERENCE_DATE (clubs redesign crests), its outline, every element, internal text, color order, orientation, and the exact number and arrangement of championship stars or other marks exactly as the club itself displays them. Use a historical crest only when the concept is explicitly historical.
3. Colors* - official primary and secondary colors with hex values from the club's brand or kit sources, which color dominates, and the main rivals' identity colors that must never appear as this club's colors.
4. Mascot or symbol* - the verified official mascot or established symbol and its correct depiction. Never invent a mascot.
5. Stadium* - current official name (sponsor names change, and competitions such as UEFA may use a different name), previous names, address, coordinates, opening year, current verified capacity with configuration and date, pitch orientation, roof type, distinctive structural features, floodlight arrangement, surrounding streets and neighboring landmarks.
6. Geography - which real landmarks, coastline, rivers, hills and skyline features are actually visible from each chosen camera, and real street names, directions and sightlines for Camera D and Camera E.
7. People* - current squad status and shirt numbers as of REFERENCE_DATE (transfers happen), legends' eras and numbers, and the captain. Never show a departed player as current.
8. Supporter culture - documented chants and rituals (never reproduce copyrighted anthem lyrics), real choreography traditions, flare and scarf culture, and fan-group names only when documented and not controversial.
9. Sensitivities - memorials and tragedies to treat respectfully or avoid, rivalry flashpoints, and political, religious or extremist symbols and slogans, which are never depicted.
10. Visible text - every word that will appear in an image, spelled exactly and kept short.

SOURCE PRIORITY\
Official club website, official channels and brand pages → official league, UEFA or FIFA pages → reputable stadium databases and major news outlets → encyclopedic cross-checks. Fan accounts, unofficial merchandise, AI-generated images and memory alone are never sources. Resolve conflicts by date and official status, and record the resolution.\

## 31 - CELEBRATION SPECTACLE ENGINE: FIREWORKS, LASERS, DRONES, FLARES, SMOKE AND CITY

Escalate across the edit rather than filling every frame: architectural light → roof fireworks ring → street flares → tifo chain → drone figure → lasers → finale. Choreograph every effect in verified club colors with one clear peak per clip. Record per-shot effects, colors and timing in CELEBRATION_PLAN.\
FIREWORKS

- Launch from defined positions: roof crown, roof edge, forecourt mortar racks behind barriers, or barges on a real waterfront.
- Mix shell types: peonies, chrysanthemums with trails, willows, crossettes, comets, mines and a cascading waterfall of sparks from the roof edge.
- Physics: rising trails, bursts at varied altitudes, spherical symmetry, sparks decaying and falling with gravity, smoke accumulating and drifting downwind and glowing when lit from inside by later bursts, and flickering colored light cast on the roof, crowd and streets.
- Exposure: bursts bright without clipping into flat blobs, farther bursts smaller, slight motion trails as in real night photography.
- Avoid identical bursts, a ring of identical vertical fountains, perfectly even spacing, bursts frozen in mid-air, bursts in front of the lens, and bursts over faces, the crest or text.

LASERS

- Compact laser emitters are visible on the roof crown or ring and synchronized.
- Beams are visible only through haze, so light haze or smoke must be present. They are thin, straight, sharp and of constant width, in club colors, forming fans, tunnels, cones and sky sweeps.
- Beams never bend, never come from the sky or from drones and always stay above the crowd, never sweeping across people.
- LZ0 shows haze and visible emitters with beams off. LZF shows the full laser formation, for example a crown of beams in the club colors above the stadium.

DRONES\
Section 15 governs. The show sits 120-300 m above and behind the stadium in clear sky, with points dense enough for a readable figure.\
FLARES AND SMOKE

- Handheld flares have a bright red or pink magnesium core, sparks, a thick smoke plume drifting downwind and intense red light on nearby faces, scarves and buildings, and are held by hands at arm's length. Smoke canisters on the ground produce club-colored smoke.
- Place them in the street, forecourt and stands, never covering featured faces, the crest or tifo text.
- Depict celebration only. Nobody throws pyrotechnics at people, onto the pitch or at police.

CITY CELEBRATION

- Streets fill with dancing crowds, flags waving from car windows, horn-honking convoys and raised scarves. Balconies carry flags and waving people.
- Verified landmarks and bridges may be lit in club colors as creative event lighting, never altered in form.
- Respect rivals: no burning or mocking of rival flags, symbols or supporters.

## 33 - REALISM BIBLE: ELIMINATING AI TELLS

No generator can guarantee 100% realism. This scan catches the tells that make viewers call an image fake, and every image passes it before acceptance.\
IMAGE SCAN

- Text and logos: no garbled letters, extra characters or mirrored crests, and as little text as possible.
- Pitch: one full-size pitch, straight lines, a correct center circle and penalty areas, two goals and a natural mowing pattern.
- Seats and tiers: continuous rows, consistent seat size, logical aisles and vomitories, no melting or merging rows.
- Structure: supports reach the ground, roof edges are continuous, no floating parts, and symmetry holds where the design is symmetric.
- People: correct scale with distance, natural poses, no fused bodies, duplicate faces or extra limbs in near-foreground subjects, hands correctly holding flags, scarves and flares, and distant crowds reading as believable texture.
- Flags, fabric and smoke: attached to poles or hands, with one consistent wind direction per frame.
- Light: every light has a source, shadows agree, the night sky stays deep blue with detail, highlights are not blown into flat white, and fireworks and flares cast colored light on nearby surfaces.
- Daytime grade: pale hazy horizon, muted true-to-life color and haze increasing with distance under the Section 01 NATURAL DAYLIGHT AERIAL GRADE. A saturated postcard sky, neon greens or uniform sharpness to the horizon fail.
- Night grade: no sunset colors in the sky, most of the frame dark, light only from real sources, no single orange or club-colored cast over the city, trees and roofs dark, a sky consistent with the daytime weather, fine shadow noise, no wet mirror pavement without rain, and fireworks varied in size, height and timing.
- Vehicles and street furniture: correct size, correct driving direction and coherent lane markings, with no melted cars.
- City: verified anchors in place, no duplicated buildings, a plausible skyline.
- Optics: depth of field consistent with the camera, fine natural sensor grain at night instead of plastic smoothness, and no oversharpening.
